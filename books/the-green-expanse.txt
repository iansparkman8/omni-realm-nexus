THE GREEN EXPANSE

Journal of Elara, Silk Merchant. Day 14 of the Spring Crossing.

The wheels of the caravan hum a steady, rhythmic tune against the packed earth of the trade road. It is morning, and the grassland stretches out before us like an ocean of emerald and gold, rippling gently as the dawn breeze brushes across the wildflower clumps. There is a profound sense of peace here, a wide-open hope that seems to rise with the sun itself. Unlike the dense thickets of the deep woods or the treacherous footing of the crags, the plains hide nothing. What you see is what is given.

Yesterday, we passed a lone field oak standing majestic against the sky. Its wide canopy provided a perfect shade for our midday rest. We sat among the hawthorn and shared bread and dried fruit. The open sky above us was a brilliant, cloudless azure, making the world feel infinite and full of possibility. My guards, usually so tense on the road, were laughing, their weapons resting idly against the cart wheels. It is hard to hold onto fear when the world is this bright.

We trade primarily in silks and spices, goods that require a steady journey to remain unspoiled. The grassland offers exactly that. The flat stones that occasionally dot the landscape serve as natural milestones, guiding us toward the bustling markets of the eastern settlements. Occasionally, we spot a herd of wild horses grazing in the distance, their coats shining in the sunlight. They are free, untethered, and they embody the spirit of this region.

I have walked many paths in this world. I have felt the biting chill of the snowcaps and the oppressive heat of the deep deserts, but it is here, in the whispering grasses, that my heart feels most at rest. There is an unspoken promise in the rolling hills: a promise that no matter how long the journey, there is always a clear path forward.

Tonight, we will camp near a patch of bramble, using the dry wood we gathered earlier for a small, warm fire. We don't need a large blaze; the nights here are gentle, kissed by a mild wind that carries the scent of blooming sea holly from a distant coast, though we are miles away. We will sleep under a canopy of stars so bright and numerous they look like scattered diamond dust.

To anyone reading this ledger in the years to come: if you find your spirit weary, if the burdens of your trade or your life weigh heavy on your shoulders, take the Grassland Road. Let the open sky remind you of your own potential. Let the hopeful sway of the grass soothe your troubled mind. Here, the world breathes with you. Here, every sunrise feels like a new beginning.
