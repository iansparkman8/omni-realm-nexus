THE TIDEWATCHER'S LOG

Notes on the sea, kept over many crossings and copied fair in Haven for the use of new captains.


THE TIDE

The sea rises and falls twice in each day. It is the same sea for every sailor, so the tide you see is the tide your friend sees.

At low water the flats come out. Walk them. Things glint in the wet sand that the sea will take back in an hour: shells, coin, now and then something older. Watch the little marker that shows the tide, and be off the flats before it turns.

At high water the beaches narrow and small boats can reach places they cannot reach at any other time.


THE SWELL

Wind makes waves and storm makes them tall. In fair weather the swell is a slow breath under the hull. In a gale it will lift a galleon like a leaf.

Rivers and lakes do not feel the tide, but they feel the rain. After a long storm they flood, and low ground beside them goes under. Do not build on the lowest bank.


ROGUE WAVES

In the worst storms a single wave sometimes stands up far higher than the rest and comes on alone. You will get a moment's warning. Turn your bow straight into it and ride up the face. Take it on the beam and you will learn why the old captains feared it.


BOTTLES

Bottles drift. Some hold a note from another shore, and some notes point somewhere worth going. Pick up every one.


THE GLOWING SEA

On some nights the water lights up blue wherever it is stirred: the wake, the oars, every breaking crest. Nobody agrees on why. Sail slowly on those nights and look behind you.


SHIPS

A small boat will get you along a coast. A sailboat will cross a strait. A galleon is thirty paces of oak with a broadside to match, and she turns like a continent. Give her room.

Airships and sky galleons cross mountains instead of going round. The air has its own pirates.


PIRATES AND WORSE

Brigs with dark sails hunt the trade lanes. Fight them broadside to broadside and keep moving. A sunk brig leaves crates. Now and then one carries something fine.

There is a ship that is not crewed by the living. She shows in fog and late at night, and she does not answer a hail. I have seen her twice. I did not follow her, and I do not tell you to.


CROSSING THE WORLD

The world has no edge. Hold one heading long enough and you will come back to the harbour you left. Captains who have done it are remembered. Carry food, mend your hull in every port, and keep this log dry.
